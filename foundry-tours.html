Foundry Tours

Art Castings of Colorado vs. Bronze Services of Loveland

Loveland has two working bronze foundries that let the public in for a firsthand look at lost-wax casting. Both charge $5, both cap tours at about eight people, and both require you to call ahead — but the day of the week, the address, and the feel of the visit differ. Here's how to pick.

Always call ahead before you drive over. These are working production foundries, not visitor attractions with fixed hours — tour availability depends on the foundry's active casting schedule. Confirm the day and time when you reserve.

Which one should you book?

Our honest take

Pick Art Castings if...

Thursday works better for your schedule, you're traveling with a school or youth group (they offer complimentary tours by arrangement), or you want the tour that's been running publicly the longest and has the most visitor reviews online.

Pick Bronze Services if...

Tuesday fits your trip, you'd rather start with a clear walkthrough of the casting process before stepping onto the floor, or you're an artist looking to discuss a commission during a weekday visit.

Only in town for one day? Either foundry gives you a genuine look at lost-wax bronze casting — the real difference is simply which day of the week you're in Loveland.

Before You Go

What to expect

1
Call to reserve. Both foundries require advance reservations — walk-ins aren't guaranteed a spot, especially for the 8-person cap.
2
Dress for a work floor. Closed-toe shoes are non-negotiable at both foundries. Skip sandals, heels, or anything open-toed.
3
Expect heat and noise. These are active foundries mid-production — conditions can be hot and loud. Safety glasses are provided at both.
4
Bring your camera. Photography is welcome at Art Castings and permitted with approval at Bronze Services — a rare chance to photograph bronze casting in progress.

Artist Spotlight: George Lundeen

The sculptor who helped found Sculpture in the Park in 1984 — and is still working out of his Loveland studio 50 years later.

Artist Spotlight: George Lundeen

If Loveland Bronze Tours profiles one artist first, George Lundeen is the strongest choice — he's one of the five sculptors who founded Sculpture in the Park in 1984, and he's still working out of his family's Loveland studio today.

In 1984, Lundeen and four other local sculptors — Dan Ostermiller, George Walbye, Fritz White, and Hollis Williford — partnered with city officials to put on an outdoor sculpture show. That first year drew 50 artists and 2,000 visitors and generated $50,000 in sales — enough to convince the city to permanently designate Benson Park as a sculpture garden the following year. More than 1,000 different artists have shown work there since, and the show now generates over $1 million in annual sales, the largest outdoor juried sculpture show in the country.

Born in 1948 in Holdrege, Nebraska, Lundeen founded his Loveland studio in 1976 — half a century of work. Southwest Art magazine has credited him with helping make "Loveland into the sculpture capital of the West." His public commissions include:

  • Benjamin Franklin — University of Pennsylvania, with additional castings across the US, Mexico, France, and England
  • Departure — Greenwood Village, Colorado
  • The Joy of Music — a five-figure bronze ensemble, with installations in Loveland and elsewhere
  • Promise of the Prairie — Holdrege, Nebraska, one of his earliest commercial successes
  • Stations of the Cross — a multi-sculpture installation at the Cloisters on the Platte near Omaha, Nebraska
  • Two statues representing Colorado and Kansas in the National Statuary Hall Collection in the U.S. Capitol

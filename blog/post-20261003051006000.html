Loveland Sculpture Garden Walking Tour: A Quiet Fall Visit

Benson Sculpture Garden gets its heaviest foot traffic in early August during Sculpture in the Park, but the garden itself is there year-round — and fall is arguably the better time to actually walk it. Fewer crowds, cooler weather, and the bronze work reads differently against turning leaves than it does in peak summer light.

The garden dates back to 1984, when five local sculptors — George Lundeen, Dan Ostermiller, George Walbye, Fritz White, and Hollis Williford — partnered with the City of Loveland to launch what became Sculpture in the Park. The city designated Benson Park as the permanent site the following year. What started as a single show has grown into a standing collection: the garden now holds 189 sculptures valued at over $5 million, and it's been recognized as one of the most significant sculpture gardens in the country.

For an actual walking route: plan a simple loop through the park rather than trying to see every piece in one pass, and go in the late afternoon if you want the best light for photos. The park is flat and paved in most areas, which makes it an easy, stroller- and wheelchair-friendly walk.

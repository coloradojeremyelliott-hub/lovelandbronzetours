Best Time to Visit Benson Sculpture Garden

Benson Sculpture Garden — photo by Ijon, CC BY-SA 4.0

Benson Sculpture Garden holds 188 permanent bronze sculptures across ten free, publicly accessible acres — and unlike a lot of Loveland's sculpture scene, it doesn't revolve around a single event weekend. Here's how the experience actually shifts through the year.

Late spring & early summer (May–June)

Green, mild, and quiet. The garden's landscaping is at its best and foundry tours run on their normal Tuesday/Thursday schedule with no show-weekend crowds to plan around.

Sculpture in the Park weekend (second weekend of August)

The busiest the garden gets all year, by a wide margin. A portion of the garden closes for the show's setup and takedown, and the juried show and sale brings visitors from well outside Colorado. If this is your reason for visiting, see the full show guide for dates, tickets, and timing tips. If it isn't, consider visiting a different week — you'll see nearly all of the permanent collection without the crowds.

Fall (September–October)

A strong, underrated window: cooler temperatures than August, still comfortable for a full walking loop, and the crowds from the show weekend have cleared out. Colorado's fall light also does the bronze patinas a lot of favors.

Winter (November–February)

The garden stays open, and snow on bronze is its own kind of photogenic — but check conditions before you go, since paths can be icy and foundry tour schedules may shift around the holidays. Call ahead to confirm a foundry tour is running before you make the trip.

Bottom line: if Sculpture in the Park itself isn't the point of your trip, almost any other week gives you the same permanent collection with a fraction of the visitors.

Pair a garden visit with the rest of downtown using the self-guided walking route, or book a foundry tour the same trip to see how the bronze in the garden actually gets made.
